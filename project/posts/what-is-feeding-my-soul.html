What Is Feeding My Soul?

A reflection on the True Vine, the voices that shape what we believe, and where true freedom is found.

We live surrounded by voices.

Some invite us to believe. Others invite us to doubt. Some point us toward Jesus Christ, while others promise that happiness and freedom can be found by leaving His commandments behind.

And many of those voices have something in common: they sound convincing.

They promise authenticity. Freedom. A life without restrictions. They tell us that no one should determine how we live, what is right or wrong, or what we should believe.

And if I am honest, sometimes those words can sound appealing.

But lately I have been thinking about a different question:

What is feeding my soul—and what kind of fruit is it producing?

The True Vine

The night before His death, Jesus Christ used a simple image to describe our relationship with Him.

He said that He is the vine and we are the branches. A branch receives from the vine what it needs to live. As long as it remains connected, it is nourished, grows, and eventually bears fruit. Separated from the vine, it cannot sustain itself.

“Abide in me.” John 15:4

I love those words because Christ is not simply asking me to know about Him. He is inviting me to remain connected to Him—to make Him my source of truth, identity, hope, strength, and life.

Elder Ulisses Soares taught about Jesus Christ as the True Vine, while warning that we live in a world filled with competing voices and philosophies that seek our attention.

Some are obviously contrary to Christ. Others are much more subtle. And some even appear to offer more freedom than He does.

That made me think about Korihor.

A Voice That Promised Freedom

In Alma 30, Korihor began teaching that there would be no Christ. But what strikes me is that his argument was more sophisticated than simply saying, “God does not exist.”

Korihor tried to convince the people that their beliefs were keeping them in bondage. Paraphrasing his arguments, he questioned why they allowed old traditions to determine how they lived, why they submitted themselves to ordinances and religious leaders, and why they believed in a Christ they had never seen.

His message was essentially:

You think you are free, but your religion is controlling you.

Then he offered an alternative. He taught that people prospered according to their own abilities and strength, and that ultimately whatever a person did was no spiritual crime.

No judgment. No eternal consequences. No need for repentance. No need for a Savior.

In modern words, his philosophy might sound something like:

Do whatever you want—and call it freedom.

Mormon describes Korihor's teachings as flattering words, and I think that description matters.

Flattery works because it tells us something we want to hear. Korihor was telling people they did not need commandments or repentance. They could determine for themselves what was right.

Does a voice become true simply because it tells me what I want to hear?

What Does It Mean to Be Free?

God has given us agency. I can believe or doubt. I can obey or disobey. I can draw closer to Christ or walk away from Him.

But there is a difference between being free to choose something and being free to determine what that choice will eventually produce in me.

This is where the metaphor of the vine becomes especially meaningful to me.

Imagine a branch saying: I don't want to be bound to this vine anymore. I want to be free.

It can separate itself. And now, in one sense, it is “free” from the vine.

But it is also disconnected from the source that gave it life.

Is that freedom—or simply separation?

Sometimes God's commandments are portrayed as the bars of a prison. But no one forces me to abide in Christ.

I choose to.

I could walk away. I could reject His teachings or listen to other voices. It is precisely because I have agency that I can say:

I choose Christ.

When I choose to keep a commandment, make a covenant, repent, or leave something behind because I want to follow Him, I have not lost my freedom.

I am using it.

And perhaps true freedom is not simply being able to say, “I can do whatever I want.”

Maybe a greater freedom is being able to say, “I don't have to do everything I want.”

I can govern my desires instead of allowing them to govern me. I can say no. I can change. I can repent. I can begin again.

I can choose Christ.

What Am I Allowing to Nourish Me?

Elder Soares described the many competing philosophies around us as vines that can become entangled with the True Vine.

I think that image fits our world remarkably well. An entangled vine does not necessarily pull a branch away all at once. At first, it simply wraps itself around it.

Perhaps spiritual drifting can happen the same way.

A video. An influencer. A philosophy. An opinion repeated over and over. An idea that once made us uncomfortable but eventually begins to feel normal.

Little by little, what I repeatedly listen to can influence what I think. What I think can shape what I believe. And what I believe eventually influences how I live.

That does not mean I should fear questions or refuse to listen to people who think differently from me. But I do think it means I should pay attention to what I continually allow to nourish my heart.

Because Korihor had been listening to a voice too.

Eventually, he admitted that he had been deceived. He explained that the adversary had told him what to teach, but then Korihor said something that stays with me.

The teachings were pleasing to his mind.

So he listened. Then he repeated them. And eventually, he believed them.

Deception does not always begin with something obviously dark. Sometimes it begins with something that simply tells me exactly what I already want to believe.

Something that makes repentance unnecessary. Something that makes my desires the highest authority. Something that assures me:

You don't need to change.

And if I listen long enough, perhaps a voice that once sounded foreign can eventually begin to sound like my own.

The Fruit Reveals the Vine

Maybe one of the simplest ways to examine the voices feeding my soul is to look at what they are producing in me.

Jesus taught that those who abide in Him bear fruit. So instead of asking only whether an idea sounds persuasive, perhaps I can ask:

What fruit is this producing in me?

Is it bringing me closer to Jesus Christ? Is there more peace, love, patience, hope, humility, forgiveness, and self-mastery?

Or am I becoming more angry, prideful, confused, critical, consumed by comparison, dependent on approval, or less interested in Christ?

The fruit reveals the vine.

Korihor's story eventually ends painfully. After everything fell apart, the voice he had followed was not there to sustain him.

Mormon draws attention to that lesson: the adversary does not support those who follow him in the end.

That creates a powerful contrast for me.

The adversary deceives. Christ teaches.

The adversary uses. Christ redeems.

The adversary promises freedom while leading us toward things that can eventually control us.

Christ asks us to surrender what enslaves us and teaches us how to become free.

One voice abandoned Korihor when there was nothing left to take from him.

Christ says:

“Abide in me.”

The Voice I Want to Know

We live in a world far noisier than Korihor's. Thousands of voices can enter our homes without ever knocking. We carry them in our pockets. They speak while we eat, while we work, before we sleep, and often from the moment we wake up.

I cannot silence every voice.

But I can choose which ones I allow to feed my soul.

I don't want to ask only: Does this sound good?

I want to ask: Where will this lead me if I keep listening?

What is it producing in my heart? What kind of person am I becoming? And most importantly:

Is it helping me abide in Christ?

Because not every voice that promises freedom can make me free. Not every philosophy that validates everything I want can give me peace. And not every vine can give life.

Jesus Christ does not ask me to abide in Him because He wants to control me.

He asks me to abide because He is the source of life.

When I am weak, He strengthens me. When I sin, He offers repentance. When I am confused, He offers truth. When I wander, He invites me to return.

A branch is not oppressed because it remains connected to the vine. It is alive because it remains connected.

And among all the voices competing for my heart, that is the voice I want to learn to recognize above all the others.

The voice of Jesus Christ.

The True Vine.

An invitation to carry with you this week

This week, pay attention to what is feeding your soul. Notice the voices you return to most often—the things you watch, read, listen to, and allow to occupy your thoughts.

You do not need to fear every unfamiliar idea or avoid honest questions. Simply look at the fruit.

Ask yourself: What is this producing in me?

Does it help you become more peaceful, loving, humble, hopeful, and connected to Jesus Christ? Or is something slowly wrapping itself around your heart and pulling you away from Him?

Among all the voices asking for your attention this week, make a little more room for the One who says: “Abide in me.”
