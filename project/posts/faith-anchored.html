Where Is My Faith Anchored?

A reflection on Job, the storms that test our faith, and finding a peace anchored not in our circumstances, but in Jesus Christ.

The story of Job awakens a deeply personal question within me:

Why am I faithful to God?

Satan claimed that Job's faithfulness depended on everything he had received. Job had wealth, health, a large family, and a prosperous life. Because of this, Satan challenged:

“Doth Job fear God for nought? Hast not thou made an hedge about him, and about his house, and about all that he hath on every side? … But put forth thine hand now, and touch all that he hath, and he will curse thee to thy face.” Job 1:9–11

In other words, his accusation was simple: Job is faithful only because he has everything. Take away his blessings, and he will stop believing.

That makes me wonder why I am faithful. Do I believe in God only because I hope to receive blessings? Would I continue trusting Him if something I consider a blessing were taken away?

Where Does My Peace Come From?

Where does the peace that we all desire come from? Does it come from financial security, physical and emotional health, or knowing that our family is doing well?

Many times, I have thought this way. As long as everything is in order, I feel peace. During those moments, it is easy to believe that God is with me. Believing, having faith, and moving forward seem much easier when life unfolds the way we expect it to.

We know that life will bring difficulties and experiences meant to teach, transform, and help us grow. But when the trial actually arrives, we may begin to wonder:

How far does this lesson need to go? How much pain is necessary? How long must it last?

Some trials appear suddenly and soon come to an end. Others continue for years. Some may even accompany us throughout much of our lives.

That is when our faith, our love for God, and what we thought we knew about Him can truly be tested. We may wonder whether all of this is worth it, whether what we believed is true, and where the love of the God who tells us He loves us can be found.

Before I was tested, what was my faith based on? Where was it anchored?

A Peace That Is Too Fragile

Which is more stable: our temporary circumstances or God's love and care?

What keeps a ship steady during a storm is not the absence of wind or the calmness of the sea, but the place where its anchor is secured.

Perhaps we sometimes discover that our faith was anchored in something too fragile: our health, money, employment, plans, relationships, or the expectation that everything had to turn out well.

These things may be good and necessary, but they are still temporary. If our peace depends entirely on them, our peace becomes just as unstable as they are.

“Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt, and where thieves break through and steal: But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven.” Matthew 6:19–20

Temporal things can change, deteriorate, or disappear. If our peace depends entirely on everything going well, it will fall when our circumstances change.

It can become like the house built upon sand. The rain descends, the floods come, and the winds blow, but the structure does not have the foundation necessary to withstand them.

The Peace That Only Christ Can Give

I love thinking about the security Jesus Christ offers. He declared:

“Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid.” John 14:27

The peace of Christ does not mean that we will never have problems. Nor does it mean that we will not experience sadness, fear, or pain.

His peace comes from knowing that even in the middle of the storm, we are not alone; that our story is still in His hands; and that, in some way we may not yet understand, everything can be all right if we remain by His side.

It moves me to know that, through the sacrifice of Christ, mercy can encircle us in the arms of safety.

“Whoso believeth in God might with surety hope for a better world … which hope cometh of faith, maketh an anchor to the souls of men, which would make them sure and steadfast.” Ether 12:4

That is the kind of peace I desire: a peace that does not depend on the sea remaining calm, but on knowing that my soul is anchored in Jesus Christ.

What Do I Do With My Pain?

I do not want to minimize the worries or afflictions that life can bring. Sometimes we may feel hopeless and want to give up. That is part of our human experience.

There will be sadness. There will be dark seasons. There will be moments when, like Job, we may feel overwhelmed by pain and bitterness.

The difference is not necessarily in what we feel, but in what we do with those feelings.

To whom do I give my pain?

Do I allow despair to convince me that everything is over, that I will never be happy again, or that peace belongs only to those whose lives are going well?

Or do I take my burdens to Christ?

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me … and ye shall find rest unto your souls.” Matthew 11:28–29

It is as though the Savior were saying: Come. Bring Me that heavy backpack you can no longer carry alone. Let Me help you.

He does not promise that every burden will disappear immediately, but He does promise rest for our souls. He may not remove the trial when we desire, but He can lighten its weight, strengthen us to carry it, and hold us when we feel we can no longer move forward.

“My son, peace be unto thy soul; thine adversity and thine afflictions shall be but a small moment; And then, if thou endure it well, God shall exalt thee.” Doctrine and Covenants 121:7–8

“A small moment” does not always feel small from our mortal perspective. Nevertheless, the promise reminds me that pain will not have the final word.

Where Will I Anchor My Faith?

Where will I anchor my faith so that my peace can endure?

Enduring peace does not mean there will never be sadness, sorrow, or affliction. It means that even when they come, we can preserve hope because we know there is a way out, a light, and a Savior.

I do not want to be faithful only because I hope to receive blessings. I want to be faithful because God is faithful.

We have a Savior who took upon Himself our pains, afflictions, temptations, sicknesses, and infirmities so that He might know perfectly how to succor us according to our needs.

Christ does not observe our suffering from a distance. He entered into it. He felt it and overcame it. Because of that, He can understand us perfectly and walk with us through every stage of our lives.

“Draw near unto me and I will draw near unto you; seek me diligently and ye shall find me.” Doctrine and Covenants 88:63

When we draw near unto Him, our lives may not immediately become easy, but they can become more bearable. We can receive strength, comfort, direction, and peace.

“I Know That My Redeemer Liveth”

Job lost his possessions, his children, his health, and the understanding of those around him. He experienced pain, confusion, and loneliness. He did not pretend that everything was all right, nor did he hide his anguish.

Nevertheless, in the midst of that suffering, he made one of the most powerful declarations of faith in scripture:

“For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth: And though after my skin worms destroy this body, yet in my flesh shall I see God.” Job 19:25–26

Job could not make that declaration because his life was going well. From a human perspective, nearly everything that had given him security was gone.

He could say it because his faith was not anchored in what he possessed, but in his Redeemer.

Job knew that his Redeemer lived. And when everything else collapsed, that truth remained firm.

The Hand That Heals

Earlier in Job, Eliphaz counseled him to seek unto God and commit his cause to Him, speaking of a God who does great and marvelous things.

“For he maketh sore, and bindeth up: he woundeth, and his hands make whole.” Job 5:18

Not all suffering means that God is correcting us, nor should we attribute every wound we receive to Him. Nevertheless, this verse expresses a truth filled with hope:

No wound lies beyond the reach of His healing hands.

He will always be our way through. He will always be the hand that heals, the love that restores joy and hope, and the embrace that comforts and consoles us.

An invitation to carry with you this week

Take some time this week to sincerely ask yourself: Where is my faith anchored?

Notice what tends to determine your peace. Is it your health, your plans, your finances, the answers you are waiting for, or the assurance that everything will work out exactly as you hope?

Then consider what it might look like to move that anchor a little deeper into Jesus Christ—to trust not only in what He gives, but in who He is.

Seek His love, His comfort, His strength, and His peace. Let Him become your security when everything else feels unstable.

And remember this unchanging truth: Our Redeemer lives. God remains faithful. His love does not change, and if we anchor our souls in Jesus Christ, the storms may shake us, but they cannot carry us away.
