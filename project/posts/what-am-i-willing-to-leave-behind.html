What Am I Willing to Leave Behind?

A reflection on conversion, mercy, and the things we may need to bury as we follow Jesus Christ.

“And this they did, it being in their view a testimony to God, and also to men, that they never would use weapons again for the shedding of man’s blood.” Alma 24:18

Recently, I read about the father of King Lamoni and a question touched my heart as I read his story:

What am I willing to leave behind to follow God?

After Aaron taught him about God, Jesus Christ, the Fall, the Atonement, and the plan of redemption, the king wanted more than simply to believe what Aaron had taught him. He wanted to know God. He was even willing to forsake his kingdom to receive the joy Aaron described. Eventually, his desire became even more powerful:

“I will give away all my sins to know thee.” Alma 22:18

I have thought a lot about those words. But as I continued reading and reached Alma 24, I realized that only a short time later we are shown what “I will give away all my sins to know thee” can actually look like when it becomes a way of life.

When Conversion Becomes a Covenant

The Anti-Nephi-Lehies had experienced a remarkable transformation. These were people who had once participated in violence and bloodshed, but after coming to know Christ and receiving forgiveness for their sins, they made a covenant with God that they would never again shed the blood of their brethren.

Then came the test. The Amalekites and Amulonites stirred up the unconverted Lamanites against them, and an army came to destroy them. The Anti-Nephi-Lehies were not seeking war. Their lives and families were in danger, and from a human perspective they had every reason to defend themselves. And yet, they refused to take up their weapons.

At first, that was difficult for me to fully understand. Would defending themselves really have been the same as the murders they had committed before their conversion? Perhaps not. But I think something deeper was happening inside them.

They had experienced the miracle of forgiveness, and they treasured that gift so deeply that they did not want to do anything that might lead them back toward the people they had once been. They had made a covenant with God, and that covenant was worth more to them than their own lives.

Because He Loveth Our Souls

Before they buried their weapons, their king explained what God had done for them:

“…because he has made these things known unto us beforehand, because he loveth our souls as well as he loveth our children; therefore, in his mercy he doth visit us by his angels, that the plan of salvation might be made known unto us as well as unto future generations.” Alma 24:14

Because He loveth our souls.

Those words change the way I see the entire story. These people were not desperately trying to earn God's forgiveness. They had already experienced His mercy. God had reached for them when they were lost. He had sent messengers to teach them, shown them the plan of salvation, taught them about Christ, offered them repentance, and forgiven them.

And He had done it because He loved their souls. He loved their children, too, and the generations that would come after them. Suddenly, burying their weapons was not simply about following a rule. It was their response to mercy. It was gratitude.

Swords Made Bright Again

Their king describes their swords as having been made bright through the blood of the Son of God (see Alma 24:15). I love that imagery. Their swords had once been stained with the blood of others. Their past was real, and they could not undo what they had done. But Christ had forgiven them. Their sins had been washed away. Their swords were, symbolically, bright again.

They treasured that cleansing so deeply that they would rather die than stain their swords again. That makes me think about my own repentance. Sometimes we become so accustomed to talking about repentance that we forget what an extraordinary miracle it actually is.

Through Jesus Christ, something stained can become clean again. Something broken can be restored. Someone who has wandered far away can come home. The past does not have to determine the future.

And when we begin to understand that, perhaps our question changes.

Instead of asking, “How close can I get to sin?” perhaps I should ask, “What can I remove from my life so I don't have to go back there again?”

They Buried Their Weapons

Then comes one of the most powerful images in the Book of Mormon. They took their swords and other weapons and buried them deep in the earth. They did not simply promise not to use them. They did not leave them somewhere nearby in case they changed their minds. They buried them.

The more I thought about that image, the more I realized that Alma 24 is not only a story about swords. Those weapons began to represent something more to me. They represented an old way of living—the violence, anger, hatred, and rebellion of the people they had been before Christ changed them.

When they buried their weapons, it was almost as if they were saying:

I am not going back.

That is where this story becomes personal for me. I may never have a sword to bury in the earth, but I have things that can become my own weapons of war: attitudes, habits, influences, or patterns that pull me away from Christ and toward the person I am trying to leave behind.

What Are My Weapons?

Maybe my weapon is pride or anger. Maybe it is resentment I refuse to release, contention I keep returning to, a habit I continually excuse, or something I watch or consume that slowly weakens my sensitivity to the Spirit.

Maybe it is a relationship, an environment, or an influence that continually pulls me backward. Maybe it is simply a distraction that has been given far more room in my heart than God has.

Am I burying my weapons—or am I only putting them away somewhere I can easily reach them again?

There is a difference. Sometimes we want repentance without removal. We want to change while keeping close the very things that repeatedly lead us back to the same place. But there may be moments when repentance asks us to dig a hole—to remove something, establish a boundary, walk away, and leave something behind.

Not because God stopped loving us when we struggled, and not because His mercy disappeared, but precisely because He loves our souls. Perhaps part of conversion is beginning to understand how precious our souls are to Him.

A Conversion Worth Protecting

The Anti-Nephi-Lehies were willing to die rather than break the covenant they had made with God. That level of conversion makes me examine my own. I hope I would be willing to give my life rather than deny Christ, but perhaps there is a more immediate question for me:

Am I willing to inconvenience myself rather than break my covenants?

Am I willing to lose an opportunity, approval, comfort, a friendship, or something I really want? It is easy to imagine giving my life for Christ in one heroic moment. Sometimes it is much harder to give Him my will in the ordinary moments of every day.

Perhaps that is where most of our weapons are actually buried—not through one dramatic sacrifice, but through hundreds of quiet decisions in which we choose Christ again.

Conversion is precious, but it must also be protected. The Anti-Nephi-Lehies had experienced Christ, received forgiveness, and made covenants, yet they still deliberately removed from their lives something that could take them backward.

How seriously am I protecting my own conversion?

Am I praying, studying the scriptures, remembering my covenants, repenting quickly, and surrounding myself with voices that lead me toward Christ? Am I feeding my testimony—or simply assuming it will always remain strong?

What Am I Willing to Leave Behind?

There is something I never want to forget in all of this: God remains faithful. His love does not disappear when we struggle. His light does not stop shining because we wander. He continues calling, forgiving, and inviting us home.

The question Alma 24 leaves in my heart is not whether He will be faithful. It is whether I will treasure what Christ has done for me enough to protect my relationship with Him.

In Alma 22, the king said, “I will give away all my sins to know thee.” In Alma 24, his people showed us what that could look like: they buried their weapons.

Perhaps that is the invitation for me now. What has remained in my hands too long? What am I still justifying? What needs to be placed deep in the earth and left there?

Not because I am terrified that God will stop loving me, but because I am beginning to understand that He loves my soul. He has shown me mercy. He has given me Christ. He has forgiven me again and again.

I want a conversion like the Anti-Nephi-Lehies—a conversion deep enough that the old weapons stay buried, strong enough that when temptation comes calling there is nothing left for me to pick up, and grateful enough to remember why I buried those things in the first place.

Christ is worth more.

An invitation to carry with you this week

As you go through this week, consider what your own “weapons” might represent. Is there a habit, resentment, distraction, influence, or attitude that keeps pulling you toward a person Christ is helping you leave behind?

You do not have to change everything at once. Perhaps there is simply one thing you can choose to lay down more deeply this week—and leave there.

Remember why: because He loves your soul. Because Christ has shown you mercy. Because He is helping you become new. And whatever He is helping you leave behind, Christ is worth more.
